What is preventive medication

Preventive medication refers to treatments taken before or after potential exposure to an illness to reduce the risk of infection. Pre-exposure medication is used regularly to offer ongoing protection, while post-exposure medication is taken shortly after a possible exposure to stop the illness from developing.

These medicines are most effective when prescribed by a healthcare professional and are often combined with regular testing, safe practices, and counseling for complete protection.

PrEP(pre-exposure prophylaxis)

What it is:What it is: PrEP is a preventive medication taken before possible exposure to HIV to greatly reduce the risk of infection.

It works by building protective levels of medicine in the body, so if HIV enters, it is blocked from taking hold and multiplying.

PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis)

What it is: PEP is a preventive medication taken after a possible exposure to HIV to reduce the risk of infection. It must be started within 72 hours and works by stopping the virus from taking hold and multiplying in the body.
